--- title: "About" permalink: "/About/" layout: post categories: media --- I’m a molecular geneticist with an M.S. in Biological Sciences from the University of Illinois Chicago, where my research focused on the genetic basis of self-incompatibility in Cactaceae. My work combines laboratory genetics (DNA extraction, PCR, and microsatellite genotyping and allele scoring on an ABI 3730xl) with computational analysis in R, Python, and Linux, including RNA-seq, differential gene expression, phylogenetics, and statistical testing of genetic models. My path began with camping and canoeing trips around Wisconsin, which grew into a broad interest in ecology, especially plants. As an undergraduate studying freshwater sciences at UW-Milwaukee, I worked in an aquatic ecology lab studying invasive fish and molluscs as well as algal physiology. That work deepened my interest in photosynthetic organisms and introduced me to aquatic plants, and later to the diversity of plant mating systems. I'm drawn to questions where genetic variation shapes biological outcomes, and to the tools that can be used to turn that variation into interpretable results. My research on plant self-incompatibility has centered on this question, testing genotype ratios against genetic models and measuring diversity across populations. I’m looking to apply my combined lab and computational background to genomics and data-driven research.

Outside of research, I play the guitar, bass, and saxophone. I love making music with friends, and we are always looking for new people to play with. I also enjoy gardening, cooking, going to the gym, fishing, and exploring!
